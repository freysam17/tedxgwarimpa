SECTION 1

FOUNDATIONS

What Is Content Strategy?

The planning, creation, delivery, and management of content that serves a defined purpose for a defined audience.

It is NOT:

  • Randomly posting quotes, flyers, or speaker photos
  • Just creating pretty graphics
  • Posting when you feel like it
If your content has no strategy, your social media is just noise. Beautiful noise, but noise.

Your Content Must Do 3 Things

For TEDx Gwarimpa specifically, every piece of content should serve one of these three goals:

1. Build Anticipation

Before events — generate excitement, awareness, and registrations.

2. Amplify Impact

During events — extend reach, capture moments, engage attendees.

3. Extend Ideas

After events — keep ideas alive, grow community, repurpose content.

The Rule: If a post doesn't serve one of these three goals, don't post it.

Content Strategy vs Social Media Management

Many teams confuse these two. Let's separate them clearly:

Content Strategy Social Media Management
Decides what to say and why Decides where to say it and how
Focuses on message and meaning Focuses on platform, timing, and engagement
Long-term thinking Day-to-day execution
The BRAIN The HANDS

You need both. Strategy without management is a plan that never happens. Management without strategy is activity without results.

SECTION 2

CONTENT STRATEGY FOR TEDx GWARIMPA

Step 1: Know Your Audience

Before you create a single post, answer this question: Who is the TEDx Gwarimpa audience on social media?

You are NOT talking to everyone. You ARE talking to:

  • Young professionals in Abuja — curious, ambitious, interested in ideas
  • Students and creatives — looking for inspiration and community
  • Potential speakers and partners — evaluating your credibility
  • The global TEDx community — discovering local events through content

Audience Persona Example

Name: Amina, 26

Location: Abuja

Occupation: Young professional, works in tech

What she wants: Inspiration, networking, exposure to new ideas

Where she spends time: Instagram, Twitter/X, LinkedIn, YouTube

What makes her follow: Consistent, intelligent, visually clean content

What makes her unfollow: Irregular posting, low-quality graphics, generic content

When you create content, you are talking to Amina. Not to a faceless crowd.

Step 2: Define Your Content Pillars

Content pillars are the 3-5 core themes that every piece of your content should fall under. They keep you focused and prevent random posting.

1

Ideas Worth Spreading

This is your core. Share powerful ideas from past TEDx talks, global TED content, or original thought-leadership posts. This pillar positions TEDx Gwarimpa as a hub for meaningful thinking.

Examples: Quote cards from speakers, short video clips, "Did You Know" posts

2

People and Stories

TEDx is about human beings with something to say. Spotlight your speakers, organizers, volunteers, and attendees. Make your community visible.

Examples: Speaker introductions, behind-the-scenes, volunteer appreciation, testimonials

3

Event Promotion

Direct promotion of upcoming events, ticket sales, registration links, and logistics. This pillar drives action.

Examples: Event announcements, countdown posts, registration reminders, venue reveals

4

Community and Culture

Content that connects TEDx Gwarimpa to Abuja, to Nigeria, to the broader culture. This makes you relatable and locally relevant.

Examples: Posts about Gwarimpa/Abuja, trending conversations, local collaborations

5

Education and Value

Teach your audience something. Give them a reason to follow you even when there is no event coming up. This keeps your page alive year-round.

Examples: Tips on public speaking, innovation, personal development

The Rule: Every single post you create must fit into one of these five pillars. If it doesn't fit, it doesn't get posted.

Step 3: Build a Content Calendar

A content calendar eliminates the biggest problem: inconsistency.

You go quiet for weeks, then suddenly flood your pages with event promotion. Your audience forgets you exist, then feels spammed.

Day Content Pillar Post Type Platform Focus
Monday Ideas Worth Spreading Quote card or idea snippet Instagram, Twitter/X
Tuesday People & Stories Speaker spotlight or team feature Instagram, LinkedIn
Wednesday Education & Value Tip, lesson, or how-to Twitter/X, LinkedIn
Thursday Community & Culture Local relevance or trending topic Twitter/X, Instagram
Friday Event Promotion Registration push or throwback All platforms
Saturday People & Stories Behind-the-scenes Instagram Stories, Twitter/X
Sunday Ideas Worth Spreading Long-form or video clip YouTube, LinkedIn, Instagram

You should be posting a minimum of 5 times per week. Anything less and the algorithm buries you.

Step 4: Content Production Workflow

Ideas mean nothing if you cannot execute. Here's a simple workflow your team should follow every single week.

Sunday Evening / Monday Morning

Planning Session (30 minutes)

Review the week's content calendar. Assign who is creating what. Confirm all assets needed (photos, videos, graphics, captions).

Monday to Wednesday

Content Creation

Graphics are designed. Captions are written. Videos are edited. Everything is prepared in advance, not on the day of posting.

Wednesday

Review and Approval

Team lead or content manager reviews all content for the rest of the week. Checks for quality, accuracy, brand consistency, and typos.

Thursday to Sunday

Scheduled Posting & Engagement

Content goes live according to the calendar. Social media manager monitors comments, replies to messages, engages with followers, tracks performance.

Key Rule: Never create content on the same day you are supposed to post it. That is how mistakes happen and quality drops.

SECTION 3

SOCIAL MEDIA MANAGEMENT

Platform Strategy

You don't need to be everywhere. You need to be excellent on the platforms that matter most.

Instagram — Your Primary Visual Platform

This is likely where most of your audience lives. Use it for polished graphics, speaker photos, event recaps, reels, and stories.

What Works

  • Carousel posts breaking down ideas from talks
  • Reels with short speaker clips (30-60 seconds)
  • Stories for behind-the-scenes, polls, countdowns
  • Clean, branded grid aesthetic

Stop Doing

  • Posting blurry event photos
  • Using inconsistent fonts and colors
  • Going weeks without posting then dumping 10 posts

Twitter/X — Your Conversation Platform

This is where you engage in real-time, share quick ideas, and participate in trending conversations.

What Works

  • Live-tweeting during events
  • Sharing speaker quotes as text posts
  • Engaging with other TEDx accounts and thought leaders
  • Short threads breaking down key ideas

Stop Doing

  • Only tweeting event links with no context
  • Ignoring replies and mentions
  • Being inactive for weeks

LinkedIn — Your Credibility Platform

This is where you attract speakers, partners, sponsors, and professional attendees.

What Works

  • Speaker announcement posts with professional bios
  • Post-event recap articles
  • Behind-the-scenes of what it takes to organize a TEDx event
  • Tagging speakers, partners, and sponsors in posts

Stop Doing

  • Copying exact content from Instagram without adapting
  • Ignoring LinkedIn entirely

YouTube — Your Archive & Discovery Platform

Every TEDx talk should live on YouTube. This is how your ideas reach people beyond Abuja, beyond Nigeria, beyond the event date.

What Works

  • Full speaker talks uploaded with proper titles, descriptions, and tags
  • Short clips (YouTube Shorts) highlighting the most powerful 60 seconds
  • Event highlight reels

Stop Doing

  • Recording talks but never uploading them
  • Poor titles like "TEDx Gwarimpa Speaker 3" instead of "How Failure Made Me a Better Leader | Jane Doe | TEDx Gwarimpa"

TEDx Gwarimpa Brand Voice

Every post should sound like it comes from the same source.

Intelligent but Accessible

You're sharing big ideas, but you're not lecturing. Talk to your audience like a smart friend, not a professor.

Warm and Inviting

TEDx is a community. Your tone should make people feel welcome.

Confident but Not Arrogant

You know the value of what you offer. You don't need to beg people to attend or follow.

Locally Rooted, Globally Minded

You're from Gwarimpa, from Abuja, from Nigeria. But your ideas are universal.

Brand Voice Example

Wrong Tone

"We are pleased to cordially invite you to the prestigious TEDx Gwarimpa event. Kindly register at the link below."

Right Tone

"The next TEDx Gwarimpa is coming. 6 speakers. 6 ideas that will challenge how you think. Abuja, are you ready? Grab your spot — link in bio."

Engagement Rules

Posting content is only 50% of social media management. The other 50% is engagement.

  1. Reply to every comment within 2 hours of posting. The first 2 hours are when the algorithm decides whether to push your content.
  2. Reply to every DM within 24 hours. If someone messages your page, they're interested. Don't lose them.
  3. Engage with other accounts proactively. Comment on posts by other TEDx events, speakers, Abuja-based creators.
  4. Ask questions in your captions. Don't just broadcast. Invite responses.
  5. Use Stories for interaction. Polls, question stickers, quizzes, countdowns drive engagement.

Metrics: What to Track

Not all numbers matter equally.

SECTION 4

FIXING COMMON PROBLEMS

Problem 1: Inconsistent Posting

The Fix: Build the content calendar as described. Batch-create content weekly. Use scheduling tools like Buffer, Later, or Meta Business Suite. No excuses.

Problem 2: Content Only During Event Season

The Fix: Your content pillars solve this. Ideas Worth Spreading, People and Stories, and Education and Value give you things to post year-round. TEDx Gwarimpa should be active 52 weeks a year.

Problem 3: Low-Quality Visuals

The Fix: Establish a brand kit. Choose 2-3 fonts, a defined color palette (use TEDx red as anchor), and a consistent layout style. Use Canva with saved brand templates.

Problem 4: No Clear Call to Action

The Fix: Every post should tell the audience what to do next. Follow us. Register now. Share this. Tag a friend. Watch the full talk. Never post without purpose and a next step.

Problem 5: Team Works in Silos

The Fix: Use a shared workspace. Google Drive folder organized by month, Trello or Notion board for planning, WhatsApp or Slack for quick coordination. Everyone should know what's being posted, when, and by whom.

Problem 6: No Post-Event Content Strategy

The Fix: The event is not the end — it's the beginning of a content cycle. One event should generate at least 30-50 pieces of content. If you're only getting 5-10 posts, you're leaving massive value on the table.

Post-Event Content Checklist

Within 48 Hours

Highlight Reel

Quick, energetic video capturing the event's best moments.

Week 1

Attendee Testimonials & Photos

Share reactions, photos, and quotes from attendees.

Week 1

Recap Article

LinkedIn article or blog post summarizing key takeaways.

Weeks 2-4

Individual Speaker Clips

Short video clips highlighting each speaker's key moments.

Weeks 2-4

Full Talks on YouTube

Properly titled, tagged, and described for discovery.

1 Year Later

Throwback Content

"One year ago" posts to resurface ideas and memories.

SECTION 5

ACTION PLAN

What to Do This Week

Five concrete actions to take immediately after this session:

1

Finalize Your Content Pillars

Take the five pillars recommended, adapt them if needed, and get the whole team aligned. Print them. Pin them. Everyone should know them.

2

Build Your First 2-Week Content Calendar

Using the template provided, plan your next 14 days of content. Assign responsibilities. Set deadlines for asset creation and review.

3

Create Your Brand Kit in Canva

Set up a shared Canva workspace with your fonts, colors, and logo. Create 5-10 reusable templates for quote cards, speaker spotlights, event announcements.

4

Audit Your Current Pages

Look at your last 20 posts across all platforms. Ask: Does this fit a pillar? Is quality consistent? Is there a CTA? Is the brand voice right?

5

Set Up a Scheduling Tool

Choose one (Buffer, Later, or Meta Business Suite) and start scheduling your first week of content. Get ahead of the posting cycle.

The 10 Rules for TEDx Gwarimpa Social Media

  • Every post must serve one of your five content pillars.
  • Post a minimum of five times per week across your active platforms.
  • Never create content on the same day you post it.
  • Speak in a voice that is intelligent, warm, confident, and locally rooted.
  • Every post must include a clear call to action.
  • Engage with your audience. Reply to comments and messages. Be present.
  • One event should produce at least 30 pieces of content.
  • Track engagement rate, saves, shares, and conversions. Ignore vanity metrics.
  • Stay active year-round, not just during event season.
  • Your content doesn't just promote events. It spreads ideas. That is the mission.
